Component 1

Company Overview

Amazon was founded in 1994 by Jeff Bezos as an online bookstore and expanded into e-commerce, marketplace services, logistics, consumer devices, media, advertising, grocery, healthcare, autonomous vehicles, and cloud computing. Its most important business transformation was the creation of Amazon Web Services, which turned Amazon’s internal technology capability into a global enterprise infrastructure platform.

Amazon’s current market position is unusual because it combines consumer scale, logistics execution, marketplace network effects, advertising growth, and enterprise cloud infrastructure. For this study trip, AWS is the most relevant lens: Amazon is trying to make AWS the default enterprise platform for building, deploying, governing, and scaling generative AI applications.

Component 2

Strategic Context — The “What”

Amazon’s AI strategy should be viewed through two lenses. First, Amazon uses AI across its own businesses: retail search and recommendation, inventory forecasting, logistics optimization, advertising, fraud detection, Alexa, robotics, and internal software development. Second, through AWS, Amazon is pushing to become the default enterprise infrastructure layer for building and operating generative AI.

The AWS AI stack has three important layers. Amazon Bedrock is AWS’s managed platform for accessing and building with multiple foundation models, including models from Anthropic, Meta, Mistral AI, Cohere, AI21, Stability AI, and Amazon’s own models. Amazon Q is AWS’s generative AI assistant family for developers, business users, contact centers, supply chain users, and AWS builders. Trainium and Inferentia are AWS-designed AI chips intended to improve the performance and cost economics of training and inference workloads.

Strategically, AWS is both responding to competitive pressure and building a differentiated enterprise AI position. Microsoft gained early AI mindshare through OpenAI and Copilot, while Amazon’s counter-position is a multi-model marketplace, a deep partnership with Anthropic, custom silicon, enterprise security, and customer choice. Amazon’s total announced investment in Anthropic reached $8 billion in 2024, and in 2026 Amazon and Anthropic announced an expanded collaboration involving significant Trainium capacity and international inference expansion.

However, the session title — Corporate Innovation Models — suggests that students should focus not only on AWS products, but also on Amazon’s internal operating mechanisms: Working Backwards, PR/FAQ documents, narrative memos, two-pizza teams, single-threaded leadership, Day 1 culture, and leadership principles. These mechanisms are Amazon’s answer to a hard question: how can a very large company keep inventing with startup-like urgency?

Executive interpretation: Amazon’s most useful lesson may not be “use AWS.” It may be that AI transformation requires mechanisms: customer-backward problem definition, disciplined narratives, accountable owners, fast experimentation, and infrastructure that can scale from prototype to production.
Component 3

Speaker Context — The “Who”

Hampton Sublett is publicly described as a mission-based Customer Evangelist and Amazon Senior Speaker focused on enterprise cloud and AI digital transformations and innovative culture. Public profile snippets also describe him as Western Region Manager for Customer Solutions Managers at AWS.

Customer Solutions Managers are important because they help large customers move from cloud ambition to operating reality. They sit at the intersection of customer success, solutions architecture, program management, change management, and executive alignment. This makes Hampton a useful speaker for understanding what actually happens when an enterprise tries to operationalize cloud and AI at scale.

His background is especially relevant for executive students because it combines public-sector and enterprise transformation experience with Amazon’s internal innovation discipline. Public sources describe earlier experience at UC Davis in IT program management and strategic solutions before AWS. That gives him a practical view of procurement, stakeholder politics, legacy systems, governance, consensus building, and organizational failure modes — the exact issues that often determine whether AI projects scale.

Perspective

Component 4

Golden Questions — The “Ask”

These questions are designed for an executive audience. They are not factual questions that can be answered by a web search; they ask the speaker to interpret Amazon’s AI strategy and innovation model in relation to Asian enterprise realities.

  1. 1 Amazon’s Working Backwards process relies on extreme clarity about the customer experience. Does the unpredictable and emergent nature of large language models challenge that framework, and how do you write a PR/FAQ for an AI product whose behavior changes with models, prompts, data, and users?
  2. 2 Working Backwards, PR/FAQ documents, and six-page narratives were designed for disciplined product thinking, often over long product cycles. With agentic AI, teams can prototype in hours. Has Amazon’s innovation methodology adapted, or are teams quietly compressing or bypassing parts of the process in practice?
  3. 3 As Asian conglomerates weigh data sovereignty, national AI strategies, and geopolitical risk, how does AWS convince state-linked enterprises to trust cloud-based AI rather than building localized on-premise small language models or using domestic cloud providers?
  4. 4 In mainland China, AWS operates through local partners and competes with strong domestic cloud providers; in ASEAN, Alibaba Cloud, Tencent Cloud, Huawei Cloud, and local sovereign-cloud initiatives are active. Which parts of Amazon’s cloud and innovation playbook transfer well to Asia, and which do not?
  5. 5 Some Amazon leadership principles, such as “Disagree and Commit” and “Frugality,” may be interpreted differently in hierarchical or status-conscious organizations. How does AWS adapt Amazon’s innovation culture for Asian clients without diluting the mechanisms that make it effective?
  6. 6 Bedrock’s multi-model strategy gives customers choice, while Copilot-like products create tighter vertical integration. For enterprises, when is model choice more valuable than a deeply integrated end-to-end AI stack?
  7. 7 Amazon has custom silicon through Trainium and Inferentia. For a non-technical board or executive committee, how should leaders evaluate whether custom AI infrastructure actually lowers total cost of ownership versus simply increasing vendor dependence?
  8. 8 For large enterprises, the hardest part of AI transformation is often not model selection but organizational adoption. What are the most common failure modes you see when enterprises try to operationalize cloud and AI at scale?
Student preparation prompt

Before the Amazon visit, choose one AI initiative your organization might pursue — customer service automation, internal knowledge assistant, fraud detection, sales forecasting, document intelligence, software development, or supply-chain optimization.

Ask: If you had to write an Amazon-style PR/FAQ before building it, who is the customer, what pain point matters most, what would make the solution meaningfully better, and what organizational risks would appear in the FAQ?
