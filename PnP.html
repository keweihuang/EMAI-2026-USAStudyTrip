Executive overview of Plug and Play

Plug and Play is best understood as a global innovation platform that links startups, corporations, investors, universities, and governments. Its distinctive model combines venture capital, accelerator programs, corporate-startup matchmaking, innovation events, and physical ecosystem infrastructure.

What it is

An innovation platform, not only an accelerator

PnP sits at the intersection of venture capital, corporate innovation, startup acceleration, and ecosystem convening. For executives, the key point is how it reduces the search, trust, and coordination costs between large firms and startups.

What it does

Curates external innovation for corporations

PnP helps corporate partners discover startups, run pilots, access emerging technologies, and learn from cross-industry innovation patterns. Its value proposition is speed: surfacing investable and pilot-ready opportunities faster than internal scanning alone.

Why it matters

A practical window into Silicon Valley execution

The visit is an opportunity to examine how venture building actually works: sourcing startups, evaluating founders, designing pilots, scaling partnerships, measuring outcomes, and converting innovation theatre into operating impact.

PnP’s venture-building flywheel

Plug and Play’s model can be viewed as a flywheel: more startups attract more corporations and investors; more corporate partners create more pilot opportunities; more pilots and investments produce more success stories; more success stories attract more founders.

History and origin story

The Plug and Play story is closely linked to Silicon Valley’s physical density, founder networks, and the evolution from real-estate-enabled startup communities to a global innovation platform.

Timeline
1980s–1990s
The “Lucky Building” roots
Saeed Amidi and the Amidi family’s Palo Alto property at 165 University Avenue became associated with early Silicon Valley startup activity. This origin story is central to PnP’s identity: proximity, serendipity, and relationship-building can become investment advantage.
2006
Plug and Play Tech Center launched in Sunnyvale
The Amidis expanded the model by acquiring a large Sunnyvale building and formally starting Plug and Play Tech Center. The organization moved beyond office space into startup acceleration, investment, corporate partnering, and events.
2010s
Vertical programs and corporate innovation
PnP scaled industry-specific programs across domains such as fintech, insurtech, mobility, health, energy, supply chain, enterprise, and sustainability. The platform became especially relevant for large firms trying to access startup innovation without building every capability internally.
2020s
Global platform era
PnP increasingly positions itself as a worldwide innovation platform connecting startups, corporations, VCs, universities, and governments. AI, climate, healthcare, mobility, financial services, enterprise software, and supply chain remain natural areas for discussion during the visit.
